Data confidentiality & safe sharing

Your business data does not need to expose your business.

A useful investigation requires relevant evidence—not unrestricted access to every record. Before any data is transferred, Pratap Labs can agree the confidentiality, purpose, minimum fields required, access method, retention period and final disposal in writing.

Confidentiality is established before analysis begins.

Pratap Labs is prepared to sign an appropriate non-disclosure agreement before receiving confidential information. A client may provide its own NDA for review, or confidentiality obligations can be incorporated into the written engagement terms.

Only data required for the agreed business question should be shared. It will be used solely for that assignment and will not be published, reused as a demonstration, sold or disclosed to another party without written authorisation.

NDA-ready · Purpose-limited · Minimum necessary data
01 / START SAFELY

No sensitive data in the first email

Describe the question, the available records and the required decision. Send only field names or a sample structure—not live customer or employee information.

02 / REPLACE IDENTIFIERS

Use codes or dummy names

Names, email addresses, account numbers and other direct identifiers can be replaced with consistent internal codes. The client can retain the code-to-name mapping and need not share it.

03 / MINIMISE

Share only what the question requires

Remove unrelated columns, unnecessary free-text fields and personal information. Aggregated or pseudonymised data may be sufficient for many commercial investigations.

04 / AGREE THE LIFECYCLE

Define access, retention and disposal

The transfer method, authorised access, working period and return or deletion of supplied data are agreed before the files are received.

A practical rule: if the analysis can be completed without knowing who a person or company is, replace the identifying details before sharing the file. Pseudonymisation reduces exposure, but the client should still treat the file as confidential whenever re-identification remains possible.

Questions before you write

Clear expectations from the beginning.

What kinds of organisations can Pratap Labs help?

Established businesses and professional firms facing a consequential question about profitability, customers, operations, inventory, demand, growth or future performance. Suitability depends on the decision and available evidence—not the industry or company size alone.

Do we need perfect data?

No. Imperfect data is common. The first task is to determine what the available information can support, what it cannot support and whether the gaps can be addressed responsibly.

Must we disclose customer names or other direct identifiers?

Usually not. Replace names and direct identifiers with stable codes or dummy labels wherever identity is not necessary for the analysis. The client can retain the mapping. The precise fields, protections and transfer method are agreed before any confidential file is shared.

Will Pratap Labs sign a non-disclosure agreement?

Yes. Pratap Labs is prepared to review and sign an appropriate NDA before receiving confidential business information. Confidentiality obligations may also be included in the written engagement agreement.

How do we communicate?

Enquiries, clarification, scoping, proposals, project correspondence and delivery are handled in writing by email. This keeps the question, assumptions and decisions precise and documented.

Will you guarantee a commercial result?

No responsible analysis can guarantee profit or growth. Pratap Labs provides evidence, explanation, priorities and practical direction while stating uncertainty and implementation dependencies clearly.

How is the fee decided?

After reviewing the question and available information, Pratap Labs replies with the recommended scope, data requirements, written deliverable, timing and professional fee. Sending an enquiry creates no obligation.

Are refunds offered?

The scope, deliverable, fee, cancellation terms and applicable legal rights are agreed in writing before work begins. Subject to applicable law, fees covering work already performed or resources specifically committed to an assignment are non-refundable.
