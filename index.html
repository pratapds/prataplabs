Welcome to the Pratap Labs retail intelligence demonstration. The information shown here is synthetic and is used only to explain the consulting approach. Begin with the executive overview to understand the overall relationship between revenue, profit, orders and units. The purpose is not to admire a headline measure, but to ask whether commercial growth and profit quality are moving together. Continue to sales trends and look for changes that deserve explanation. A change in performance may reflect demand, basket value, product mix, promotions, availability or returns, so the next step is to test those explanations rather than assume a cause. In product performance, compare demand with contribution and identify products that appear strong on revenue but weak on margin quality. In store performance, compare locations facing similar conditions and investigate differences in assortment, availability, returns and commercial execution. Customer analysis helps examine purchase behaviour and concentration without exposing personal identities. Inventory analysis brings together availability, excess stock and financial exposure so operational action can be prioritised. Return analysis separates different causes because each cause requires a different response. The management conclusion is to turn each important finding into a clear decision, an accountable owner and a way to evaluate what changed. This is how Pratap Labs uses business analysis and data science to guide a practical, evidence-based decision.

Professional principles

The reasoning should be as trustworthy as the result.

Good consulting makes the evidence, assumptions, uncertainty and decision ownership visible.

Evidence before claims

Findings are connected to relevant evidence, and limitations are stated clearly.

Data minimisation

Only data needed for the agreed question should be used, under documented access and handling rules.

Client data stays client data

Client information is not reused for another organisation without explicit written permission.

Explainable recommendations

Decision-makers can inspect the reasoning, challenge assumptions and choose whether to act.

Human accountability

Analysis supports a decision; it does not replace the responsibility of the decision owner.

Clear engagement terms

Scope, responsibilities, data handling, deliverables and commercial terms are agreed in writing.

Questions worth investigating

Analysis is useful when it changes a decision.

The right question depends on the business context. These examples show how reporting can be reframed into a decision.

Operations

Where do operational activity and financial outcomes disagree?

The issue: strong activity can conceal weak commercial quality.

The analysis: connect operational measures with contribution, capacity, service and risk.

The decision: identify what should be protected, changed or investigated further.

Customers

Which customer behaviours should influence the next business action?

The issue: averages can hide meaningful differences in behaviour and value.

The analysis: compare relevant groups while respecting privacy and business context.

The decision: choose an action that can be explained, tested and reviewed.
